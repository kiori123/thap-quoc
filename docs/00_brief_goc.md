# THẬP QUỐC

## Game Concept & Narrative Design Brief

**Version:** 1.0\
**Purpose:** Source-of-truth brief for Claude to develop the concept
into a coherent game narrative and production-ready design proposal.\
**Genre direction:** Dark Eastern Fantasy, mythology, political drama,
character-driven mystery.\
**Working title:** *Thập Quốc: Nhất Tự Quy Lai*\
**Language:** Vietnamese source concept; Claude may propose
English-facing terminology where useful, but must preserve the
Vietnamese names and their intended meanings.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Instructions to Claude

You are acting as a senior narrative designer and worldbuilding editor.
Your task is to develop the game concept below without replacing its
identity with a different story.

Work in this order:

1.  Audit the existing premise for contradictions, weak causality, and
    unanswered questions.
2.  Establish a consistent set of world rules that make the central
    twist inevitable in retrospect.
3.  Build the protagonist's personal motivation and emotional arc.
4.  Design the ten kingdoms and ten Tự as distinct ideological,
    political, and emotional conflicts.
5.  Design the narrative progression from the opening to the ending,
    including foreshadowing and reveals.
6.  Present any major creative decisions explicitly. Distinguish
    established canon from your proposals.
7.  Only after the narrative foundation is coherent, recommend how it
    could translate into game structure.

Do not jump straight into combat systems, skill trees, monetization,
live-service systems, or technical implementation. The immediate
priority is a strong story and world foundation.

## Non-negotiable writing rules

-   Preserve the core premise, the first Tự, the ten disciples, the ten
    kingdoms, the old man, and the final returning slash.
-   Do not turn the old man into an illusion, hallucination, imaginary
    friend, or a simple projection. His relationship with the
    protagonist must have been emotionally real.
-   Do not make all ten Tự interchangeable villains. Each must have a
    distinct worldview, history, governing method, moral compromise, and
    relationship to the first Tự.
-   Do not resolve atrocities with a cheap redemption. Understanding a
    character does not erase the harm they caused.
-   Do not make the protagonist a generic Chosen One whose actions
    happen because a prophecy says so.
-   Avoid convenient magic that exists only to explain the ending. Every
    important supernatural rule should be established before it becomes
    essential.
-   Keep the first Tự's compassion distinct from passivity. He can
    understand his disciples without excusing their actions.
-   Avoid melodramatic exposition. Prefer meaningful actions, recurring
    details, contradictions, and character choices.
-   Do not add lore simply to make the world larger. Every addition
    should strengthen character, theme, conflict, or causality.
-   Do not use em dashes in the final prose.
-   Avoid generic AI fantasy language, excessive grandiose titles, and
    repetitive phrases such as "the fate of the world" unless the
    context earns them.

When proposing changes, use these labels: - **CANON:** directly
established by this brief. - **PROPOSAL:** a recommended addition that
can be accepted, revised, or rejected. - **OPEN QUESTION:** a decision
that genuinely needs the creator's input.

# 2. High Concept

In a world where humanity's unchecked desires have become monstrous, a
rebellious god gives up his divinity and immortality to defend
humankind. Known to mortals only as **Tự**, he trains ten disciples to
carry on his work.

The disciples initially protect humanity together. Over time, each comes
to believe that their own ideal is the only way to save the world. They
divide the land into ten kingdoms and become the rulers known as the
**Thập Tự**, ranked from Nhất Tự to Thập Tự by strength.

Centuries later, an orphan grows up in a remote village under the care
of a humble old man who never smiles. Whenever the protagonist condemns
the cruelty of the Thập Tự, the old man tries to understand the people
they once were. The protagonist believes this is naïveté and sets out to
confront the rulers and prove that some people no longer deserve
understanding.

The journey gradually reveals that the ten rulers are not merely ten
monsters to defeat. They are ten different answers to the same question:
how much freedom should people have to choose their own fate, even when
they may choose wrongly?

After gathering the legacy of all ten Tự, the protagonist unlocks a
hidden technique: one single slash directed toward the place where the
journey began. The slash cuts through the old home. The protagonist
returns to find that no one in the village remembers the old man.

The truth is not that the old man never existed. He is the first Tự, the
teacher who gave the ten rulers their first lessons and has waited for
them to return for centuries. The final slash is the convergence of the
ten disciples' legacies, their first true acknowledgment that they have
strayed from the purpose for which they were trained. It reaches the
teacher and releases him from the bond that kept him waiting.

For the first time in centuries, the old man smiles.

The story is not about proving that every villain can be forgiven. It is
about the difference between understanding a person and excusing their
choices, between protecting people and controlling them, and between
being powerful enough to rule the world and humble enough to find one's
way home.

# 3. Core Canon

## 3.1 The Heavenly Court and the Nghiệt

**CANON**

In the ancient world, the Heavenly Court governs the cosmic order.
Humanity is given intelligence, will, and the capacity to choose its own
future.

Over generations, human desire grows beyond restraint. People seek not
merely prosperity but ownership of everything, not merely safety but
absolute control, not merely long life but immortality. The Heavenly
Court concludes that humanity can no longer control its own desires.

The Court's judgment gives physical form to destructive desires. These
beings are called **Nghiệt**.

Nghiệt are not ordinary animals or monsters from another dimension. They
embody desires such as greed, envy, arrogance, hatred, domination, and
obsession. They emerge where those desires become concentrated and can
corrupt the places they inhabit.

The Heavenly Court calls this judgment purification.

**Narrative principle:** Nghiệt should not be treated as proof that
every desire is evil. Desire can motivate love, survival, ambition, art,
and sacrifice. The danger begins when a desire becomes absolute and
denies the humanity of others.

**OPEN QUESTIONS for later development** - Are Nghiệt created directly
by divine judgment, generated by collective human desire, or produced by
an interaction between the two? - Can a Nghiệt be destroyed permanently,
or can similar desires create another one? - Can a person resist a
Nghiệt without eliminating the desire that gave rise to it? - Does the
Heavenly Court understand the full consequences of its judgment?

Claude should recommend one internally consistent model rather than
leaving all alternatives active in the final world bible.

## 3.2 The First Tự

**CANON**

One god rejects the Heavenly Court's judgment. He believes humanity
cannot be defined only by its worst impulses. He has witnessed
compassion, sacrifice, and people choosing to protect others even when
doing so costs them dearly.

He gives up his divine position and descends to the mortal world. The
price is immortality. He retains extraordinary power, but he can age,
suffer wounds, and die.

Humans do not know that he was a god. They know only that he stands
beside them when the heavens have abandoned them. They call him **Tự**.

He does not establish an empire, demand worship, or seek absolute
authority. His central belief is that people deserve the chance to
choose their own fate. That does not mean every choice is good or free
of consequences.

As his mortal life approaches its end, he realizes he cannot protect
humanity forever. He chooses ten disciples and teaches them the skills,
knowledge, and principles he has accumulated.

**Important characterization** - He is compassionate, but not naïve
about cruelty. - He believes people can change, but does not believe
change erases responsibility. - He does not force his disciples to
follow him forever. - His defining failure is not that he loved them too
much. It is that he could teach them principles but could not make their
future choices for them.

## 3.3 The Ten Disciples and the Thập Tự

**CANON**

The ten disciples come from different backgrounds and possess different
temperaments and ideals. At first, they work together to protect people
from Nghiệt, rebuild settlements, and restore civilization.

As their power grows, each becomes convinced that their own way of
saving humanity is the only viable way. They separate, establish ten
kingdoms, and become the rulers known collectively as the **Thập Tự**.

Each kingdom has only one reigning Tự at a time. The titles **Nhất Tự,
Nhị Tự, Tam Tự, Tứ Tự, Ngũ Tự, Lục Tự, Thất Tự, Bát Tự, Cửu Tự, and Thập
Tự** represent their order of strength, from strongest to weakest.

The ranking reflects strength, not necessarily wealth, territory,
military size, or diplomatic influence.

**CANON: Nhất Tự** - He is the strongest of the ten. - He is also the
most deeply corrupted by power. - He may have begun as the disciple most
committed to protecting humanity. - His corruption grows from the belief
that if he becomes strong enough, he can prevent every tragedy. - He
gradually confuses protecting people with controlling them. - He does
not abandon his original ideal. He turns that ideal into a justification
for unlimited authority.

**Narrative principle:** Each Tự must retain a trace of the person they
once were. This should be demonstrated through specific memories,
policies, relationships, or choices, not only through explanatory
flashbacks.

# 4. The Protagonist

## 4.1 Starting point

**CANON**

The protagonist is an orphan raised by an old man in a remote village.
The old man lives simply, performs ordinary chores, treats the
protagonist with care, and never smiles.

He never gives a clear account of his past. When the protagonist speaks
angrily about the Thập Tự, the old man does not deny their crimes.
Instead, he tries to explain that people do not become who they are in a
single moment and that even terrible rulers may once have wanted to
protect others.

The protagonist finds this frustrating. To them, the old man seems too
forgiving of people who cause suffering.

## 4.2 Personal motivation

**PROPOSAL: recommended direction**

The protagonist begins the journey to prove the old man wrong.

They have witnessed or personally experienced the consequences of a
ruler's decisions. They believe that understanding the motives of the
powerful too often becomes an excuse for leaving victims to suffer. They
want to confront the Thập Tự directly and establish whether the old
man's belief in their remaining humanity has any value.

This motivation is personal, understandable, and imperfect. It gives the
protagonist a reason to travel beyond collecting powers or fulfilling a
prophecy.

The journey should not simply convert the protagonist from anger to
forgiveness. A stronger arc is:

1.  **Certainty:** The protagonist believes evil people must be
    confronted and stopped.
2.  **Complication:** Each kingdom reveals why people accepted its
    system and what genuine need it once served.
3.  **Moral pressure:** The protagonist sees that overthrowing a ruler
    does not automatically solve the conditions that created the ruler's
    power.
4.  **Discernment:** The protagonist learns to understand motives
    without excusing choices, and to oppose cruelty without assuming
    every person is beyond change.
5.  **Final choice:** When given access to the combined legacy of the
    ten Tự, the protagonist must decide what that power is for.

Do not make the protagonist passive. They must make consequential
decisions, sometimes wrong ones, and bear the consequences.

## 4.3 Avoid the Chosen One problem

The protagonist should not be secretly destined by an ancient prophecy
to save the world. Their role should emerge from the decisions they make
and the relationships they form.

**OPEN QUESTION:** Why can the protagonist carry or gather the legacies
of all ten Tự? Claude should propose a rule rooted in the world's
established metaphysics and the protagonist's choices. It must not be
explained only by "they are special."

# 5. The Old Man and the Mystery

## 5.1 What the audience sees

The old man is ordinary in appearance and habit. He grows food, repairs
the house, cooks, worries about the protagonist, and listens to stories.
He is not openly mystical. His kindness should be specific and lived-in
rather than expressed through constant wise speeches.

He never smiles. The reason must not be stated early.

He speaks about the Thập Tự with an unusual mixture of familiarity,
grief, and restraint. He remembers their humanity but does not deny the
harm they have done.

## 5.2 The reveal

**CANON**

The old man is the first Tự: the god who gave up divinity and trained
the ten disciples.

The village does not remember him. After the final slash, the
protagonist returns to find the house cut in two and cannot persuade the
villagers that the old man ever lived with them. They remember the
protagonist as someone who lived alone.

The story must not dismiss the old man as imaginary. His care, the
protagonist's memories, and their relationship must remain real within
the story.

## 5.3 Make the mystery fair

The reveal should feel surprising at first and inevitable in retrospect.
Claude must create a foreshadowing plan with details that have an
ordinary explanation before the reveal and a deeper meaning afterward.

Possible categories of foreshadowing to evaluate, not automatically
accept: - The old man's knowledge of the disciples' habits, values, and
small personal preferences. - His careful avoidance of certain
questions. - His unusual reaction to news from the kingdoms. - Objects
or household routines that echo the disciples' shared past. - A village
history that subtly contradicts the protagonist's memories. - The old
man's ability to remain calm around dangers that frighten others,
without openly displaying supernatural power.

Do not overuse clues. The player should be able to miss some details and
still understand the reveal.

## 5.4 Why does nobody remember him?

This is a crucial unresolved rule. The final version must explain why
the villagers cannot remember the old man without erasing the emotional
reality of the relationship.

Claude should compare possible explanations and recommend one. It may
involve the first Tự's renunciation, the cost of remaining in the mortal
world, a binding that kept him outside ordinary history, or another
coherent mechanism. These are possibilities, not canon.

The rule must: - Be established indirectly before the reveal. - Explain
why the protagonist remembers him. - Explain why the house remains
physically present. - Explain what changes when the final slash is
released. - Avoid functioning as a convenient memory wipe used only for
a twist.

# 6. The Ten Kingdom Journey

The game should combine two narrative layers.

## Outer journey: resistance

The protagonist travels across the ten kingdoms, encounters injustice,
challenges the rulers' systems, and confronts the consequences of their
decisions. The conflict must have real stakes. People suffer,
institutions resist change, and defeating a ruler does not magically
repair society.

## Inner journey: discovery

The protagonist gradually learns the history of the ten disciples and
the first Tự. Their understanding of the old man's words changes as they
encounter evidence of the disciples' earlier selves.

The protagonist should not receive a simple message that "everyone is
good deep down." The more difficult lesson is that a person's history
may explain their actions without making those actions acceptable.

## Prevent repetitive kingdom arcs

Do not use the same sequence for every kingdom: arrive, see cruelty,
defeat the ruler, obtain a mark, leave.

Each kingdom should differ in: - The type of conflict: political,
social, personal, philosophical, or supernatural. - The protagonist's
role: investigator, outsider, reluctant ally, mediator, rebel, or direct
challenger. - The degree to which the ruler is directly responsible for
the crisis. - The type of decision that resolves or changes the
situation. - The information revealed about the old Tự and the ten
disciples. - The cost of success.

Some conflicts may end in a duel, but not all should. A ruler might lose
legitimacy, a policy might be dismantled, a truth might be exposed, or
the protagonist might have to choose between two imperfect outcomes.

## Required narrative design table

For each of the ten kingdoms, produce a table containing: 1. Kingdom and
Tự title. 2. Core ideal and its original humane purpose. 3. Present-day
distortion. 4. The central local conflict. 5. The ruler's personal
history and original wound or fear. 6. The ruler's connection to the
first Tự. 7. The Nghiệt or supernatural threat involved. 8. The
protagonist's initial assumption. 9. The revelation that complicates
that assumption. 10. The protagonist's consequential choice. 11. The
outcome and its cost. 12. The legacy, clue, or mark obtained. 13. The
foreshadowing planted for the ending.

Do not finalize all ten biographies before checking that their stories
create a varied and escalating overall journey.

# 7. Nhất Tự: The Strongest Disciple

Nhất Tự must be more than the strongest boss or the last ruler standing.
He is the clearest ideological opposite of the first Tự.

## The first Tự believes:

Humanity deserves the freedom to choose its own fate, even though people
can choose wrongly and hurt one another. Protection must not become
ownership.

## Nhất Tự believes:

Humanity cannot be trusted with the freedom to keep making catastrophic
mistakes. If he is strong enough to prevent war, cruelty, and disaster,
then he has a duty to control the choices that lead to them.

His reasoning should be persuasive enough that the audience understands
why he came to believe it. He should have prevented real disasters.
People should have legitimate reasons to support him. The horror is that
he has turned prevention into domination.

His central contradiction: - He understands the teacher's lesson better
than most of the other disciples. - He has also constructed the most
complete argument for rejecting it. - He does not see himself as evil. -
He believes that being responsible for the world means accepting the
right to decide for everyone.

His eventual participation in the final returning slash must not
instantly absolve him. The slash represents an acknowledgment that he
has gone too far, not proof that his crimes have been undone.

**OPEN QUESTION:** Determine whether Nhất Tự is the final major
antagonist, a late-game ideological opponent, or one part of a larger
conflict. The story should not assume that defeating the strongest
person automatically resolves the world's political and moral problems.

# 8. The Hidden Skill: The Returning Slash

## 8.1 Canonical event

After overcoming the trials connected to all ten Tự, the protagonist
gathers ten marks or legacies and unlocks a hidden technique. When
activated, it produces one single slash directed toward the place where
the protagonist's journey began.

The slash cuts through the old house. The protagonist returns and
discovers that the old man is gone and that the village remembers no
such person.

The slash is the convergence of all ten disciples' legacies. It is their
acknowledgment that they have strayed from the path their teacher
intended. In a symbolic and supernatural sense, they have finally turned
back toward home.

The slash cuts the bond that has kept the first Tự waiting. It does not
kill him. It allows him to let go.

For the first time in centuries, he smiles.

## 8.2 Causality that must be solved

The current idea is emotionally powerful but requires rules. Claude must
answer all of the following before finalizing the plot:

1.  Why can the protagonist gather all ten legacies?
2.  What exactly is a mark or legacy: power, memory, a vow, an aspect of
    the teacher's art, or something else?
3.  Why does gathering the ten legacies unlock one slash rather than
    simply making the protagonist stronger?
4.  Why is the slash directed toward the starting village?
5.  Why does the slash cut the house and release the teacher instead of
    injuring him?
6.  Why does the village no longer remember him?
7.  How are the disciples involved in the slash? Is their involvement a
    literal act of will, a resonance of their legacies, or a symbolic
    consequence of the protagonist's choices?
8.  What does the protagonist choose that allows the ten legacies to
    unite?
9.  Why could the first Tự not release himself earlier?
10. What consequences remain after the slash?

Do not answer these with unrelated magical exceptions. Prefer a small
set of world rules that explain several story events at once.

## 8.3 Recommended thematic direction

**PROPOSAL**

The hidden technique should not be a reward for simply defeating ten
rulers. It should become possible because the protagonist has
encountered all ten distorted ideals and refuses to use their combined
power to impose a single ideal on everyone.

The ten legacies are not merely ten energy sources. They represent ten
partial interpretations of the teacher's lessons. Alone, each has become
distorted by the disciple who carries it. Their convergence becomes
possible only when the protagonist stops treating one perspective as the
complete answer.

The slash is not an attack aimed at the old man. It is a return: the ten
paths, divided for centuries, finally converge on their origin. Its
effect on the house should be governed by the same supernatural rule
that explains the first Tự's long waiting and the village's missing
memory.

This is a recommended direction, not a finalized mechanic. Claude must
test it for consistency and explain the exact rules in plain language.

## 8.4 Emotional staging

Do not over-explain the ending through a long speech or an exposition
dump.

The sequence should allow the player to experience: 1. The release of
the hidden slash. 2. The inexplicable pull to return home. 3. The
damaged house and the old man's absence. 4. The villagers' confusion. 5.
The realization that the protagonist's memories are not shared by anyone
else. 6. The truth about the first Tự. 7. The meaning of the ten
legacies converging. 8. The first smile.

The final smile should not mean that every crime is forgiven or the
world is repaired. It means that the teacher has finally received an
answer to the wait that defined his life.

# 9. Themes and Moral Boundaries

## Primary themes

-   Desire and human nature.
-   Ideals corrupted by absolute certainty.
-   The difference between protection and control.
-   The right to choose, including the possibility of choosing wrongly.
-   Understanding a person without excusing their actions.
-   The burden of a teacher's legacy.
-   The difference between power and responsibility.
-   The longing to return home after becoming someone unrecognizable.

## Moral boundaries for the narrative

-   Do not imply that victims owe forgiveness to those who harmed them.
-   Do not treat good intentions as sufficient justification for
    atrocities.
-   Do not make the first Tự's compassion equivalent to tolerating
    injustice.
-   Do not make every rebel morally pure or every institution
    irredeemable.
-   Do not solve systemic problems solely by removing one ruler.
-   Do not let the final emotional reunion erase the people harmed by
    the Thập Tự.
-   Allow some consequences to remain unresolved. A meaningful ending
    can offer emotional closure without pretending the world has become
    perfect.

A concise table with: - Established canon. - Current ambiguity or

## Deliverable C: Character arcs

Develop: - The protagonist. - The first Tự / old man. - Nhất Tự. - The

For each major character, define: - What they want. - What they fear. -
What they believe. - What they misunderstand. - What they did that

## Deliverable D: Ten-kingdom narrative matrix

Use the 13-field table specified in Section 6. Ensure variety,
escalation, and a clear relationship between each kingdom's ideology and
its Nghiệt.

## Deliverable E: Full story outline

Provide: - Opening and inciting incident. - Why the protagonist leaves
home. - Major turning points. - The order and rationale for visiting the
kingdoms. - Midpoint revelation. - Escalation involving Nhất Tự. - The
protagonist's decisive moral choice. - The hidden slash. - The return to
the village. - The old man's reveal and final smile. - The political and
personal aftermath.

The story outline must explain cause and effect, not only list events.

## Deliverable F: Foreshadowing and reveal map

Create a table showing: - Clue. - First appearance. - Initial
interpretation. - True meaning. - When the player can understand it. -
Risk of revealing too much too early.

## Deliverable G: Ending stress test

Answer: - What would make the ending feel unearned? - What must the
player know before the reveal? - What should remain mysterious? - Which
consequences must not disappear after the ending? - How does the ending
change the meaning of the opening?

## Deliverable H: Game adaptation recommendations

Only after A to G are coherent, suggest a high-level game structure. Do
not assume a genre or combat format that has not been decided. Offer
alternatives if the story could support different game forms, and
explain the narrative trade-offs of each.

# 12. Final Direction

The heart of **Thập Quốc** is not simply a god, ten kingdoms, ten
powerful rulers, or a hidden ultimate attack.

It is the contrast between two ideas:

The first Tự believes that people deserve the freedom to choose their
fate, even when they make terrible choices.

Nhất Tự believes that people must be controlled because their freedom
allows them to hurt one another.

Between these beliefs stands an orphan who initially wants to prove that
the old man is wrong. The journey forces the protagonist to confront the
failures of the Thập Tự without losing the ability to see them as human
beings.

And at the center of the world is a teacher who has waited for
centuries, not for ten kings to surrender their thrones, but for ten
lost disciples to remember where they came from.

**The ending should not say that everything is forgiven. It should say
that, at last, they remembered the way home.**

Begin with Deliverable A. Do not rush into implementation. If a critical
decision cannot be resolved from the canon, present the strongest
recommendation and explain why, instead of hiding the uncertainty.
